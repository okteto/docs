An Okteto backup holds what you need to recover your instance after an unexpected failure, and to prepare for disaster recovery: the Okteto metadata and, optionally, your users' Namespaces.

## Okteto Metadata

The most important resources to back up to recover an Okteto instance are:

- **Service Accounts**: Okteto creates a Kubernetes service account for every user who logs in. Back up all the service accounts in the `okteto` namespace with the label `dev.okteto.com: "true"`, so your users don't need to recreate their Okteto accounts.
- **Secrets**: Kubernetes secrets in the `okteto` namespace store Okteto variables, tokens, certificates, SSH key pairs, and other secrets. Backing them up saves you from reconfiguring all of them during a recovery.
- **Custom Resources**: the custom resources in the `okteto` namespace for the [Okteto CRDs](self-hosted/manage/crds.mdx), which have the label `app.kubernetes.io/part-of: okteto`. Okteto uses CRDs to store your Catalog, container registry credentials, and more.

Okteto recommends backing up these resources every 24 hours.

## Recovery Process

There are two recovery processes:

- **Okteto Metadata recovery process**: recovers all the users' metadata, tokens, variables, secrets, and the custom resources listed in [Okteto Metadata](self-hosted/manage/backup-restore.mdx#okteto-metadata). It doesn't recover users' Namespaces, Preview Environments, or volumes.
- **Okteto Metadata and Users' Namespaces recovery process**: recovers the Okteto metadata and all users' Namespaces, Preview Environments, and volumes. It's a full recovery of the Okteto instance.

Namespaces are ephemeral in Okteto, so Okteto recommends the Okteto Metadata recovery process: your developers recreate their Namespaces as they need them.

### Okteto Metadata Recovery Process

This is the fastest and simplest recovery process. It does **not** restore users' Namespaces or application data.

1. Back up the [Okteto Metadata](self-hosted/manage/backup-restore.mdx#okteto-metadata) in your `okteto` namespace.
1. Create a new cluster.
1. Reinstall the Okteto Helm chart in the new cluster, with the same configuration as your previous Okteto instance.
1. Restore the [Okteto Metadata](self-hosted/manage/backup-restore.mdx#okteto-metadata) in the `okteto` namespace of the new cluster.
1. The Ingress-Nginx service in the new cluster gets a new load balancer. Point your DNS records to the IP of the new load balancer.
1. Once the restore is complete and you've verified that the new cluster works correctly, delete the old cluster.

### Users and Namespaces Recovery

This option takes more time and needs an external tool to back up all your Namespaces:

1. Back up users' Namespaces with a tool like [Velero](https://velero.io/). All these Namespaces have the label `dev.okteto.com: "true"`.
1. Back up the [Okteto Metadata](self-hosted/manage/backup-restore.mdx#okteto-metadata) in your `okteto` namespace.
1. Create a new cluster.
1. Restore users' Namespaces in the new cluster with a tool like [Velero](https://velero.io/), before you reinstall Okteto.
1. Reinstall the Okteto Helm chart in the new cluster, with the same configuration as your previous Okteto instance.
1. Restore the [Okteto Metadata](self-hosted/manage/backup-restore.mdx#okteto-metadata) in the `okteto` namespace of the new cluster.
1. The Ingress-Nginx service in the new cluster gets a new load balancer. Point your DNS records to the IP of the new load balancer.
1. Once the restore is complete and you've verified that the new cluster works correctly, delete the old cluster.

:::note
Private registry configurations aren't restored until you reinstall the Helm chart. Until then, some pods may fail to pull images from the Okteto Registry.
:::
