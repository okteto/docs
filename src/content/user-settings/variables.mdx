**Settings > Variables** stores your own [Okteto Variables](application-environments/okteto-variables.mdx). Okteto passes them to every application environment you deploy, from the Okteto CLI or the Okteto UI: your Okteto Manifest expands them with the `${VARIABLE}` syntax, and the commands of its `deploy`, `destroy`, and `test` sections get them as environment variables. Use them for values that only apply to you, such as your own API keys. Nobody else on your team sees them.

## Adding a Variable

1. In the Okteto UI, open **Settings > Variables**.
1. Click **Add Variable**.
1. Enter the **Name** and the **Value** of the variable, and click **Add Variable**. The name can only have letters, numbers, underscores, and hyphens: Okteto turns spaces into underscores and removes the other characters.

   <Image
     src={require("@site/static/img/variables-add.png").default}
     alt="The Add Variable dialog, with the Name and Value fields"
     width="450"
   />

If a variable with the same name already exists, Okteto asks you to confirm before it replaces its value. Once you add a variable, the table masks its value.

To delete a variable, click **Remove** next to it and confirm. You can't recover a deleted variable.

## Precedence

When two kinds of Okteto Variables have the same name, the more specific one wins. Your variables override the [Admin Variables](admin/admin-variables.mdx) that an Admin defines for every user. In turn, a variable that you pass when you deploy, a [Catalog](admin/catalog.mdx#add-environment-variables) variable, an environment variable of your terminal, or a variable of the `.env` file next to your Okteto Manifest overrides yours. When an environment variable of your terminal overrides one of your Okteto Variables, the Okteto CLI warns you. [Okteto Variables](application-environments/okteto-variables.mdx#precedence) lists the full order.

Okteto Variables don't reach your Development Containers. To set variables in a Development Container, use the [`environment`](reference/okteto-manifest.mdx#environment-string-optional) field of the `dev` section of your Okteto Manifest.

## Reserved Variables

`OKTETO_TOKEN`, `OKTETO_URL`, `OKTETO_CONTEXT`, and `OKTETO_NAMESPACE` are reserved: they configure the [Okteto CLI context](user-settings/personal-access-tokens.mdx#using-a-token-with-the-okteto-cli) in scripts and CI pipelines. You can't use them as the name of a variable in your Settings, in Admin Variables, in Catalog variables, or in the variables that you pass when you deploy.

## Masking

The Okteto CLI masks the values of your variables in the logs of your deployments and tests, so your secrets don't leak into a log that someone else reads. It only masks values longer than 5 characters, so short values such as `true` or `false` stay readable.
